# Style Sheet — *Forty Seconds*

## Manuscript Information

- **Title:** Forty Seconds
- **Author:** Thomas Butler
- **Genre/Subgenre:** Detective noir / literary fable
- **Target audience:** Adult literary fiction readers; readers with familiarity with noir conventions; readers likely to appreciate absurdist premises played straight
- **Word count (initial):** Approximately 3,800 words (story only, not including front/back matter)
- **Date of developmental edit:** 2026-03-18

---

## Author's Stated Intentions

- **Tone/mood goal:** Classic detective noir — dry, spare, slightly sardonic narration; serious register applied consistently to absurdist premise
- **Intentional style choices (per CLAUDE.md):**
  - Stuffed animals in a dead-serious noir register — absurdity is a feature, not a bug; do not suggest "straightening" the premise
  - Lyric bleed mechanic: characters infected by the album unknowingly speak/think in *Pop* lyrics; bleeds are planted subtly, not flagged by narration
  - Final observation window ambiguity: intentionally unresolvable dual reading (corruption reveling vs. fragment performing)
  - Tom's corridor lyric bleed (T1): the only Tom bleed; the recognition scene is central to the ending
  - Execution scene is never named as such; reader infers from clinical detail (table, straps, tube, metal stand)
  - Coda in italics: three lines, present and past tense mix, ending with "Let this story serve as a warning"
  - Running gag: zebra remains on Tom's head throughout the scissors fight
  - Francois's final speech: no lyric that would resolve the dual reading
- **Off-limits / previously fixed (per CLAUDE.md):**
  - Vivian Plume was not floor-dwelling before her murder
  - Stuffed sheep "Margaret" does not exist; do not reintroduce
  - Tom has no all-caps outburst after confession; response to "I strangled her" is a quiet "Why?"
  - Two "dark nothing" window formulations are fixed and distinct
  - The two alarm systems in Tom's corridor reflection are named explicitly: the instinct and the rumors
  - Francois does not explain his own split mind; Tom deduces it
  - Scissors attack interrupts the deduction, does not follow it
  - "Something moved behind Francois's eyes" is the closing beat of Section 4, not a prelude to the scissors attack
  - "The Do you ever wonder paragraph" has been removed; do not reinstate
  - The flash of terror, the third-person slip, and the "Something moved behind Francois's eyes" beats are intentional foreshadowing — do not remove

---

## Narrative Fundamentals

- **POV:** Close third person, Tom's POV throughout; exception: Section 1 opens in Francois's POV for the discovery scene, then transitions to third-person narration before handing off to Tom in Section 2
- **Tense:** Past tense throughout; exceptions: italicised coda ("The boombox stays locked up" — present tense for ongoing imperative)
- **Structure:** Five numbered sections + italicised coda; five-act shape; each section functions as a structural act
- **Central dramatic question:** Who killed Vivian Plume — and why did the killer hire Tom to investigate?
- **Underlying dramatic question (revealed at end):** Has Tom already been compromised by the corruption, and if so, when did it begin?
- **Timeline:** Events span approximately one week in 1997

## Character Registry

### PI Tom
- **Description:** The boy in the photos; wears yellow flannel shirt; appears throughout
- **Role:** Protagonist and point-of-view character for most of the story
- **Defining traits:** Twenty years of instinct; analytical, dry, reluctant; processes via questions not reactions; suppresses emotional disclosure
- **Desire:** Stay away from anything connected to the corruption
- **Obstacle:** Manipulated into involvement by Francois / the fragment
- **Arc:** Reluctant refusal → capitulation → successful investigation → corridor reckoning / discovery of possible earlier infection
- **Key line:** *"Fine. But I do this my way."*
- **Status at close:** Uncertain; possible corruption unknown

### Francois (Stripes)
- **Description:** Stuffed zebra toy; striped; the subject of photos 48000–48005
- **Role:** Client / antagonist / puzzle / victim
- **Defining traits:** Split between corrupted mind and dimming uncorrupted fragment; strategic under the appearance of devastation; speaks in lyric bleeds
- **Desire:** (Corruption) to remain free; (Fragment) to be discovered and stopped
- **Obstacle:** (Fragment) cannot overpower the corruption directly; can only find loopholes
- **Arc:** Discoverer of Vivian → persuader of Tom → confessor → attacker → table scene; fragment extinguished at close of Section 4
- **Key line:** *"Francois deserves that much."* / *"I'm already gone. Felt that way all along."*
- **Status at close:** Executed (inferred from observation window scene)

### Vivian Plume
- **Description:** Stuffed ostrich in a teal collar; female; appears in photos 48006 (face down) and 48007 (strangled)
- **Role:** Victim; witness to the corruption; the story's moral weight
- **Defining traits:** Observant; frightened but unable to act; kept a list of Francois's strange words
- **Desire:** Unknown; presumably wanted to help Francois and didn't know how
- **Status:** Dead before the story begins
- **Key artifact:** The list in her desk drawer — the story's most important piece of physical evidence
- **Epitaph (coda):** *"Vivian Plume was buried on a Wednesday. It rained."*

- **The apartment / rooms:** Where Vivian died; contains the boombox, the scissors, Vivian's desk, the bedpost; investigative setting for Section 3

## Open Issues for Revision (per developmental edit)

1. **Priority 1** — Breath between confession and deduction in Section 3: consider one beat between "He filed it next to the other things he couldn't name" and "He already knew, of course" to allow the confession's weight to register before the analytical pivot.

2. **Priority 2** — "He already knew, of course" transition: consider clarifying the distinction between what Tom already knew (the mechanism) and what he is working out (its application to Francois's specific case).

3. **Priority 3** — "Behind him, the observation window went dark": confirm whether this sentence is communicating the intended signal (execution complete) to readers who may not have fully decoded the execution scene context.

4. **Priority 4 (optional)** — Tom's personal stakes in the hiring scene: one sentence about what the corruption would cost Tom personally could deepen the corridor moment from intellectually terrifying to also personally terrifying. Author's call.
